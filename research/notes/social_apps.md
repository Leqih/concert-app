# Competitive Teardown: Concert-Companion and IRL Friendship Apps (for "Plus One")

Research date: 2026-09-28. Scope: North America first. Every number has a date and a source link. Where a source is a competitor's own blog (FestivalMates, Muse) or a low-rigor survey (DatingNews), that is flagged.

---

## 1. Product teardown (13 products)

### Takeaway
The closest analogue to Plus One, Radiate, already combines event-based buddy finding with a PayPal-protected ticket marketplace. Its main problems are content moderation (sexual profiles, spam) and "random" swipe matching. The products with the most traction (Timeleft, Pie, 222, Partiful, Posh) use small groups or events, not 1:1 swipes. The biggest failure (IRL) shows how badly event-based social apps can inflate user numbers.

### Cited Findings

**Radiate (concert/festival social app, US; the most direct competitor)**
- What it does: connects people going to the same festival or concert. It has a "Hey" DM mechanic and event-specific group chats, and it partners with artist tours. Founders are Michael Tom and Philip Butler. It was on Product Hunt on May 27, 2019, and expanded to local electronic-music events in January 2023. It calls itself "not a dating app" but is often compared to Tinder Festival Mode — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radiate_(app))
- Features on the App Store listing: event group chats and forums; a "secure PayPal-backed ticket and clothing marketplace with buyer/seller protection"; a 3D social map of events and attendees; dating-style profile swiping. Rating is 4.9/5 from about 31K ratings (read September 2026). Last update seen was v30.8 on August 30, 2024 ("improved the Marketplace") — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/radiate/id939284774)
- Monetization: "Radiate Glow" subscription at $9.99/1 mo, $24.99/3 mo, $34.99/6 mo and $99.99/yr, plus "Heydar Tokens" at $7.99–$26.99 — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/radiate/id939284774)
- Complaints: one review dated January 8, 2025 says the landing page is "essentially being porn 90% of the time". Others mention crashes and missing shows — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/radiate/id939284774)
- A competitor's analysis (FestivalMates founder, April 7, 2026; biased source) says Radiate's user base is "overwhelmingly North American" (EDC, Ultra, Electric Forest, Bonnaroo). It lists these complaints: "swipe-based matching feels random", spam and fake profiles, no Spotify or music-taste integration, missing lineup and set-time data, and weak squad-formation tools — [FestivalMates blog](https://www.festivalmates.com/blog/radiate-alternatives-2026)
- The marketplace is PayPal-protected for "tickets and more". The fetched page had no launch date or fee details — [Edmtrain](https://edmtrain.com/news/radiate-launches-paypal-protected-social-marketplace-for-safely-buying-and-selling-tickets-and-more-652)

**Tinder Festival Mode (feature inside a dating app)**
- Launched April 14, 2022. Members select festivals they plan to attend and can match with other attendees up to one month before the event. It covered more than 20 festivals in 10 countries (Bonnaroo, EDC LV, Gov Ball, Lollapalooza) and had a general "Festival Goers" space. Tinder's own survey of singles aged 18–39 found: 31% planned to attend a festival or concert that year; 64% enjoy meeting new people at live music; 61% became friends or more with people they met at festivals or concerts; 75% say connecting online first reduces first-meeting pressure — [Tinder Press Room](https://www.tinderpressroom.com/2022-04-14-Tinder-Kicks-Off-A-Return-to-IRL-with-Festival-Mode-TM)

**Beatmatch (music-taste matching, dating + friends)**
- Founded by Chudi Iregbulem (ex-AWS, DJ). It syncs with Spotify or Apple Music and matches people with similar taste. It was in the 2021 Visible Hands pre-seed fellowship, with a Seattle beta planned for January 2022. Vinylly was a similar earlier app — [GeekWire, 2021](https://www.geekwire.com/2021/changing-the-tune-on-dating-apps-tech-vet-wants-to-connect-people-through-their-taste-in-music/)
- Its LinkedIn page shows it was a Techstars Music company. The App Store name is now "Beatmatch: Do More Together", which suggests a shift toward activities and friends — [LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/company/joinbeatmatch); [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/beatmatch-music-dating-app/id1540216913)

**Muse (findmuse.app)**
- Described on its own blog (self-promotional): users can "Wave" 1:1 at people going to the same show, or join event-specific group chats and crews. Matching uses a music-taste quiz and past gigs. It shows how many people are going solo to each event, which it frames as solving the "coincidence problem". Listed cities include NYC, LA, Toronto, Mexico City, London, Berlin and others. Core features are free; users are 18+. No user numbers are disclosed — [Muse blog](https://findmuse.app/blog/music-life/how-to-find-people-to-go-to-concerts-with)

**EventBuddie**
- "Never go to a concert alone again." Users match with nearby fans going to the same concert, chat, and follow artists. It is iPhone-only, free and contains ads. Released September 15, 2025; last updated August 11, 2026 (v2.18). As of September 2026 it did not have enough ratings to show a score, which suggests very low traction. The only safety feature listed is an 18+ age rating — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/eventbuddie/id6748877517)
- Other small entrants exist (ConcertBuddy.net, GigBuddy.app, Showmate), but no traction data was found for them — [ConcertBuddy](https://www.concertbuddy.net/en); [GigBuddy](https://gigbuddy.app/)

**Timeleft (dinners with 6 strangers; the strongest group-model evidence)**
- Format: dinners with "5 strangers" (6 people per table) lasting about 3 hours. As of August 2025 it had 150,000 monthly dinner participants and 200+ active cities, down from a 2024 peak of 320+. Revenue went from €125 in the first week (May 2023) to €20K MRR (December 2023), €1M per month about 16 months after launch, and €18M ARR (August 2025). It has about 100 employees — [Tim Frin Substack](https://timfrin.substack.com/p/inside-timelefts-journey-to-connecting)
- A San Francisco feature reported: 300+ cities in 60 countries; 11,500+ strangers every Wednesday; about $16 per dinner ticket or $26/month unlimited (food not included); 68% of users are women; participants said meeting in a group felt "safer" than dating apps — [Alta Journal](https://www.altaonline.com/dispatches/a63924701/strangers-in-the-night/)
- It is subscription-only (1-, 3- and 6-month plans), and food is paid at the venue — [Timeleft Help Center](https://help.timeleft.com/hc/en-150/articles/27963865308060-What-Subscription-Plans-Are-Available); [Subscription-only article](https://help.timeleft.com/hc/en-150/articles/17677297227164-Subscription-Only-Experience)

**Pie (Andy Dunn; AI-matched groups for events)**
- Groups of 6 are matched by an AI personality quiz and get a pre-event group chat. Formats include "Coffee with Strangers". Pie pays organizers to host events. As of March 4, 2025 it had 130K+ MAU and a $11.5M Series A, and operated only in San Francisco and Chicago — [TechCrunch, Mar 2025](https://techcrunch.com/2025/03/04/andy-dunns-new-app-pie-uses-ai-to-help-you-make-friends)

**222 (AI-matched group experiences, no profiles, DMs or swiping)**
- Matches people by personality test into dinners, comedy shows and similar outings. Cities: LA, NYC, SF, Chicago, Toronto, DC, London. Raised a $3.6M seed (April 2023, General Catalyst) and a $10.1M Series A (December 2025, Upfront). Has 16 employees — [SV Post](https://svpost.com/articles/222-10m-series-a/); [TechCrunch 2022](https://techcrunch.com/2022/11/14/2440702/)

**Bumble For Friends / BFF + Geneva**
- Relaunched September 18, 2025 on top of Geneva, which Bumble acquired in 2024. It added a Groups tab (chat rooms, hangouts, in-app calendar, communities from small up to thousands of members) and kept 1:1 matching and DMs. Group discovery was delayed to February 2026. Geneva shut down as a standalone app. Geneva had "not generated any revenue as of June 30" 2025. Bumble research: 47% want more friends to do activities with — [TechCrunch, Sep 2025](https://techcrunch.com/2025/09/18/bumble-bffs-revamped-app-is-here-focusing-on-friend-groups-and-community-building)
- Bumble (the dating app) launched government-ID verification with a badge on March 17, 2025, including in the US, Canada and Mexico. Users can filter matches by photo or ID verification. The launch also included "Review Before You Send" and "Share Date". The article does not say whether BFF has ID verification — [Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/91299913/bumble-adds-an-id-verification-feature-to-win-over-cautious-daters)

**IRL (event-based group messaging; failure case)**
- Gen Z event-organizing and event group-chat app. Raised $200M+, including a $170M Series C led by SoftBank in 2021 at a $1.17B valuation. It claimed 20M MAU. The CEO was suspended in April 2023 amid an SEC investigation. It shut down on June 26, 2023 after the board found that 95% of its users were "automated or from bots" — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/06/26/irl-shut-down-fake-users/)

**Meetup (interest groups; the incumbent)**
- 60M+ users (earlier figures: 8M in 2010, 25.5M in 2013). Ownership: WeWork bought it for about $156M in November 2017; sold to AlleyCorp in March 2020 "reportedly at a substantial loss"; Bending Spoons acquired it in January 2024. The business model charges organizers. A 2019 test of a $2-per-RSVP fee for attendees caused a "furious" backlash — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meetup); [Built In NYC](https://www.builtinnyc.com/articles/meetup-bending-spoons-acquisition-20240131)

**Partiful (event invites; social graph around events)**
- Freemium, with core features free. It makes money from add-ons such as Instacart Group Order (a $5 host fee plus a percentage of the order). Valued at $140M (2025) with $27.3M raised, including a $20M Series A1 led by a16z in November 2022. Over 90% of downloads come from the US. Competitors include Apple Invites (February 2025) and WhatsApp Events (May 2024) — [Sacra](https://sacra.com/c/partiful/). Named Google's "best app" of 2024 — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/11/18/partiful-is-googles-best-app-of-2024/)

**Posh (small-event ticketing + discovery)**
- As of July 24, 2024: $22M Series A ($31M total), 2M registered users, $95M lifetime GMV, about 5,000 events per month, and about 350K monthly bookings. Hosts pay nothing; attendees pay 10% + $0.99 per ticket. The share of ticket sales coming through the in-app marketplace grew from 6% (January) to 12% (June 2024) — [Digital Music News](https://www.digitalmusicnews.com/2024/07/24/posh-raises-22-million-with-small-events-platform-model/)

**Incumbent music platforms (discovery, but no buddy-finding)**
- Spotify: "Concerts Near You" playlist (March 2025) and venue following (October 2025). Tickets are sold through Live Nation and Ticketmaster. TechCrunch mentioned no social or friends feature — [TechCrunch, Oct 2025](https://techcrunch.com/2025/10/20/spotify-now-lets-you-follow-venues-to-get-details-on-upcoming-concerts/)
- Songkick: Suno bought it from Warner Music Group on November 27, 2025 and intends to keep it running as a fan destination — [IQ Magazine](https://www.iqmagazine.com/2025/11/ai-music-company-suno-acquires-songkick-from-wmg/)
- Bandsintown: has historically offered social features (connecting with friends, seeing who's going) — [VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/mobile/bandsintown-makes-concert-discovery-more-social-with-updated-mobile-apps); [Bandsintown support](https://bandsintown.desk.com/customer/en/portal/articles/2961424-how-do-i-connect-with-friends-on-the-app-). No current (2025–26) stranger-matching feature was found.

### Inferences
- There is a real gap between the major platforms (Spotify, Ticketmaster, Bandsintown, Songkick), which handle discovery and ticketing but not stranger-matching, and the niche buddy apps, which have little traction apart from Radiate. Plus One's pitch of a face-value swap plus a crew is close to Radiate's marketplace plus group chats. Plus One would need to win on trust (verification, moderation, face-value enforcement) and on group or crew formation, which is exactly where critics say Radiate is weak.
- Swipe-based 1:1 concert apps tend to drift toward dating and hookups (the Radiate reviews, Tinder Festival Mode). That drift is a trust problem for women seeking friends.

### Gaps
- No verified download or MAU figures for Radiate, Beatmatch, Muse, GigMate or ConcertBuddy were found. No Sensor Tower or Appfigures data was accessed.
- No sources were fetched for Hinge concert features, Ticketmaster/Live Nation social features, Discord fan servers, or GigMate. I did not find evidence that Ticketmaster has a buddy-matching feature.
- The Radiate marketplace launch date and fees are unknown.
- No reliable data was found on Bumble BFF's standalone MAU after the relaunch.

## 2. Matching models: 1:1 swipe vs small group vs event chat

### Takeaway
The companies with the strongest disclosed traction and revenue all use **curated small groups of about 6** (Timeleft at €18M ARR; Pie at 130K MAU; 222 with a $10M Series A). Bumble moved BFF from 1:1 swiping to groups in 2025. 1:1 swipe concert apps show weak or unverifiable traction, and complaints about them focus on randomness and sexualization.

### Cited Findings
- Timeleft: 6-person dinners; 150K monthly participants and €18M ARR (August 2025) — [Tim Frin](https://timfrin.substack.com/p/inside-timelefts-journey-to-connecting)
- Pie: groups of 6 with a pre-event group chat; 130K+ MAU (March 2025) — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/03/04/andy-dunns-new-app-pie-uses-ai-to-help-you-make-friends)
- 222: "no profiles, DMs or swiping"; group experiences; $10.1M Series A (December 2025) — [SV Post](https://svpost.com/articles/222-10m-series-a/)
- Bumble BFF relaunched with a focus on groups and communities (September 2025) — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/09/18/bumble-bffs-revamped-app-is-here-focusing-on-friend-groups-and-community-building)
- Radiate's swipe matching is criticized as "random" (competitor-authored source) — [FestivalMates](https://www.festivalmates.com/blog/radiate-alternatives-2026)
- Timeleft participants described a group as "safer" than 1:1 dating-style meetings — [Alta Journal](https://www.altaonline.com/dispatches/a63924701/strangers-in-the-night/)
- Event-based group chat at scale (IRL) could not show real engagement: 95% of users were bots — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/06/26/irl-shut-down-fake-users/)

### Inferences
- For Plus One, a hybrid looks best: an event-anchored "crew" of about 4–6 people going to the same show (as in Timeleft and Pie), with 1:1 limited to the ticket swap itself. Open, large event chats risk spam and fake accounts; small curated groups add structure and safety.
- A small group also reduces the need to find a single perfect match, which lowers the density required per event.

### Gaps
- No published retention or repeat-rate comparison between 1:1 and group formats was found. Timeleft's figure of 2.2 experiences per month per user is the only engagement metric — [Tim Frin](https://timfrin.substack.com/p/inside-timelefts-journey-to-connecting).

## 3. Safety demands, especially from women

### Takeaway
Women are much more worried than men about meeting strangers from apps, and harassment on dating apps is widespread. The main answers in the market are ID and photo verification badges with filters, message screening, sharing meeting details with friends, group formats and public venues. Timeleft's user base is 68% women, which suggests group formats attract women.

### Cited Findings
- Pew (survey fielded July 2022): 57% of women vs 41% of men say dating apps are not a safe way to meet people. Among women under 50 who have used dating apps: 56% received unwanted explicit content, 43% had unwanted continued contact, 11% faced physical threats, and 66% experienced at least one of these — [Pew Research, Feb 2023](https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/2023/02/02/from-looking-for-love-to-swiping-the-field-online-dating-in-the-u-s/)
- DatingNews.com survey (August 23, 2024; sample size not disclosed, so treat with caution): 91% of single women worry about dating safety; 75% say apps must improve safety; 97% take precautions such as public places and sharing their location — [Global Dating Insights](https://www.globaldatinginsights.com/news/91-of-women-want-better-dating-app-safety-features-survey-finds/)
- Bumble ID verification badge plus filtering, "Review Before You Send" and "Share Date" (March 2025, US/CA/MX) — [Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/91299913/bumble-adds-an-id-verification-feature-to-win-over-cautious-daters)
- Timeleft is 68% women — [Alta Journal](https://www.altaonline.com/dispatches/a63924701/strangers-in-the-night/)
- Radiate's moderation failures (sexual content on the landing page) show up in App Store reviews (January 2025) — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/radiate/id939284774)
- Muse's recommended practices: keep chat in-app, meet at the venue, share plans, control your own transport, 18+ only — [Muse blog](https://findmuse.app/blog/music-life/how-to-find-people-to-go-to-concerts-with)

### Inferences
- Plus One's safety stack could include: ID verification (also useful for trust in ticket swaps), a women-only or gender-preference crew option, meeting at the venue, sharing the meet-up with a friend, reporting and blocking, and a clear non-dating positioning. The Radiate and Tinder examples show that without moderation, concert-matching drifts toward hookups.

### Gaps
- No survey specifically about safety concerns when meeting *friends* (as opposed to dates) from apps was found.
- No data was found on how common women-only options are in friendship apps, or how they are used.

## 4. Cold start and density

### Takeaway
The successful apps create density by batching demand in time and place: fixed-day group events, minimum signup thresholds per city, and a few cities at a time. Anchoring on existing events or ticketing (Posh, Radiate at big festivals, Tinder Festival Mode's list of 20 festivals) is the other proven approach.

### Cited Findings
- Timeleft needed 151 signups before launching in a city (scarcity plus a viable minimum), and charged from the start to filter for real intent — [Startup Spells](https://startupspells.com/p/how-timeleft-solved-the-chicken-and-egg-problem)
- Timeleft validated with a Typeform, WhatsApp and Stripe, and no app. It expanded internationally only through paid ads, with no local staff. It went to 320+ cities in 2024, then cut back to 200+, suggesting some cities could not sustain density — [Tim Frin](https://timfrin.substack.com/p/inside-timelefts-journey-to-connecting)
- Timeleft runs everything on the same night: 11,500+ strangers every Wednesday — [Alta Journal](https://www.altaonline.com/dispatches/a63924701/strangers-in-the-night/)
- Pie launched city by city (SF, then Chicago) and pays organizers to host, so it supplies events itself — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/03/04/andy-dunns-new-app-pie-uses-ai-to-help-you-make-friends); [TechCrunch Nov 2024](https://techcrunch.com/2024/11/19/irl-social-app-pie-is-coming-to-sf-to-make-you-less-lonely)
- Tinder Festival Mode concentrated matching on a curated list of 20+ festivals, opening one month before each — [Tinder](https://www.tinderpressroom.com/2022-04-14-Tinder-Kicks-Off-A-Return-to-IRL-with-Festival-Mode-TM)
- Radiate's density is concentrated around large EDM festivals (EDC, Ultra, Electric Forest, Bonnaroo) — [FestivalMates](https://www.festivalmates.com/blog/radiate-alternatives-2026)
- Posh grew its share of sales through the in-app marketplace from 6% to 12% in 2024 by starting with ticketing for hosts — [DMN](https://www.digitalmusicnews.com/2024/07/24/posh-raises-22-million-with-small-events-platform-model/)
- Muse addresses density by showing how many people are going solo to each show (self-reported) — [Muse](https://findmuse.app/blog/music-life/how-to-find-people-to-go-to-concerts-with)

### Inferences
- Plus One should start with one metro and one scene (for example, arena pop tours or a single festival), where fan communities already cluster. Ticket swaps can be the utility that brings users in before social density exists. Fake-user risk (IRL) means real, verified activity per event matters more than headline signups.

### Gaps
- No published data on the minimum number of attendees per event needed for matching was found.

## 5. Monetization benchmarks

### Takeaway
Subscriptions tied to real-world events work (Timeleft: about $26/month, €18M ARR). Transaction fees work when there is a ticket (Posh: 10% + $0.99). Pure community apps struggle to make money (Geneva had no revenue by mid-2025; the Meetup RSVP-fee backlash). Radiate uses a dating-style mix of subscriptions and tokens.

### Cited Findings
- Timeleft: about $16 per dinner or $26/month unlimited; subscription-only; €18M ARR in August 2025 — [Alta](https://www.altaonline.com/dispatches/a63924701/strangers-in-the-night/); [Tim Frin](https://timfrin.substack.com/p/inside-timelefts-journey-to-connecting)
- Radiate Glow at $9.99/mo to $99.99/yr, plus tokens at $7.99–$26.99 — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/radiate/id939284774)
- Posh: 10% + $0.99 per ticket paid by attendees; $95M lifetime GMV (July 2024) — [DMN](https://www.digitalmusicnews.com/2024/07/24/posh-raises-22-million-with-small-events-platform-model/)
- Partiful: core features free; revenue from add-on commerce (Instacart Group Order) — [Sacra](https://sacra.com/c/partiful/)
- Geneva had no revenue as of June 30, 2025 and was folded into BFF — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/09/18/bumble-bffs-revamped-app-is-here-focusing-on-friend-groups-and-community-building)
- Meetup charges organizers; the 2019 $2-per-RSVP fee for attendees caused a backlash — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meetup)
- FestivalMates Pro costs €4.99/mo or €49.99/yr — [FestivalMates](https://www.festivalmates.com/blog/radiate-alternatives-2026)

### Inferences
- For Plus One, a face-value swap limits how much can be charged on ticket transactions: a small flat buyer-protection fee, like Posh's $0.99, is more defensible than a percentage. The more likely revenue lines are a premium "crew" subscription or verification tier, or artist and venue partnerships.

### Gaps
- No conversion benchmarks (free to paid) for friendship apps were found.

## 6. Demand: loneliness and the desire to make friends through shared activities

### Takeaway
Loneliness is highest among young adults: 67% of Gen Z say they struggle with it (Cigna 2025). There is direct evidence of demand for friends to do activities with (Bumble: 47%) and for meeting people at live music (Tinder: 64% of singles 18–39).

### Cited Findings
- Cigna "Loneliness in America 2025" (surveys fielded May 29–June 14, 2024; n = 7,500+ US adults): 52% struggle with loneliness overall; Gen Z 67%; Boomers 44%; young unpaid caregivers aged 18–34, 72% — [Advisory Board summary](https://www.advisory.com/daily-briefing/2025/06/19/loneliness); [Cigna report PDF](https://filecache.mediaroom.com/mr5mr_thecignagroup/183661/2025-loneliness-in-america-report-the-cigna-group.pdf)
- Pew (July 17–23, 2023; n = 5,057): 8% of US adults say they have no close friends — [Pew](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2023/10/12/what-does-friendship-look-like-in-america/)
- Bumble research: 47% want more friends to do activities with (September 2025) — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/09/18/bumble-bffs-revamped-app-is-here-focusing-on-friend-groups-and-community-building)
- Tinder survey of singles aged 18–39 (2022): 64% enjoy meeting new people at live music events; 61% became friends or more with people they met at festivals or concerts — [Tinder](https://www.tinderpressroom.com/2022-04-14-Tinder-Kicks-Off-A-Return-to-IRL-with-Festival-Mode-TM)

### Inferences
- The demand case is strong in general but indirect for concerts specifically. The only concert-specific numbers come from a Tinder survey of singles, and Tinder has a commercial interest in them.

### Gaps
- I did not get the Pew breakdown by age for people with no close friends, or Pew data on where friends are met. Gallup and APA data were not retrieved.
- No independent survey on how many fans skip concerts because they have no one to go with was found.
