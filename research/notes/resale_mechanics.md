# Face-Value and Fan-to-Fan Ticket Resale in North America: Mechanics, Fees, Rules, and Why Face-Value Startups Struggled (as of Sept 28, 2026)

## Q1. Platform-by-platform comparison (price cap, fees, payout, guarantee, transfer, eligibility, geography, limits)

### Takeaway
Face-value resale in North America mostly lives inside the primary ticketer: Ticketmaster Face Value Exchange (free to sell, fixed at price paid, artist/organizer opt-in) and the DICE wait list (organizer-enabled, seller refunded). Independent face-value sites (Tixel, Twickets, CashorTrade) take fees that together often run about 10-20% on top for buyers, and they depend on whatever transfer method the source ticketer allows. Open marketplaces (StubHub, SeatGeek, Vivid) have no cap and charge sellers about 10-15%.

### Cited Findings
- **TM Face Value Exchange:** the listing price "is automatically set at the total price you paid" and "tickets may only be listed at the total price paid". It is free to list and sell. Payout comes "typically within 7 business days after the event". Sellers need a US bank account and SSN/ITIN, and tickets must be bought on Ticketmaster. Listings run up to one hour after the event starts, sales are first come, first served, and unsold tickets are returned. — [Ticketmaster Help](https://help.ticketmaster.com/hc/en-us/articles/9781464415249-How-does-Ticketmaster-s-Face-Value-Exchange-work) (accessed Sept 2026)
- TM FVE is opt-in by artist/organizer only and is often mobile-only once enabled; standard TM resale uses market pricing with a seller fee. — [The Ticket Blog, 17 Sept 2026](https://www.theticketblog.com/post/fan-to-fan-resale) (secondary, low detail)
- TM markets FVE to artists as a B2B product. — [Ticketmaster Business](https://business.ticketmaster.com/face-value-exchange/)
- **Tixel USA:** buyer service fee 6.9% (7.9% via Auto-Purchase), minimum $4.50, plus a "Credit Card/Banking fee of 4.5%". Seller fee 5.9%, minimum $4.50. Listing and waitlist are free; sales tax passes to the buyer. The page was last updated 24 May 2023. — [Tixel Help: Fees USA](https://tixelhelp.zendesk.com/hc/en-au/articles/18836653161113-Tixel-Fees-USA)
- **CashorTrade:** buyers pay a 10% platform fee (non-Gold) plus a 3-5% card fee. Gold members pay no platform fee, except 5% (capped at $250) on purchases over $2,500. Listing and selling are free. Sellers face a penalty of "up to 150%" for failed delivery and a $25 chargeback fee. Gold members get "Trader's Protection with 100% ticket coverage". — [CashorTrade Help](https://help.cashortrade.org/hc/en-us/articles/12918261373069-Understanding-Fees-on-CashorTrade)
- **Twickets US:** "The only service to cap prices at face value (+fees)". It is "Supported by over 200 artists", and US listings include Iron Maiden (Prudential Center) and George Strait (Soldier Field). — [twickets.live/en/us](https://www.twickets.live/en/us). The US launch was first announced in 2017. — [TicketNews, June 2017](https://www.ticketnews.com/2017/06/twickets-launching-united-states/)
- **DICE wait list:** if the ticket sells, the original holder gets a full refund. Each buyer in line gets a timed reservation window. The feature is only available if "the event organiser" enables it. Unsold tickets can be transferred to a friend "if that option is available for the show". — [DICE Help](https://dicefm.zendesk.com/hc/en-gb/articles/19958073128849-The-wait-list-explained)
- **AXS:** the UK help center explains a 10% resale cap ([AXS UK](https://support.axs.com/hc/en-gb/articles/360033290353-Why-is-there-a-10-cap)). - **Seller fees for open marketplaces (third-party compilation, 12 July 2026):** StubHub about 15% plus processing, SeatGeek flat 10%, Vivid 10%, Ticketmaster about 10-15%, AXS capped at 10%, TickPick 15% with $0 buyer fees. The same source says "SeatGeek/Vivid buyer-fee ranges are third-party estimates (not official)". — [SellTicketsFast](https://sellticketsfast.com/ticket-selling-fees-compared/)
- **FTC v. StubHub (filed 9 Apr 2026):** alleges violations of the Fees Rule. The FTC says StubHub hid mandatory fees on the selection, info and checkout screens and cites one example where the total was "25% higher than the price initially advertised". It also alleges StubHub delayed compliance around the NFL schedule release. — [FTC complaint PDF](https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/StubHub-Complaint.pdf)

### Inferences
- The only "fee-free face value" options (TM FVE, DICE wait list) belong to the primary ticketer and need artist/organizer opt-in. Independent face-value platforms have to charge buyers about 10-15%, which erodes the "face value" promise.
- A Plus One swap flow that routes through an official exchange (deep-link to TM FVE or DICE) avoids fee and trust problems. A standalone escrow model has to copy CashorTrade's penalty and escrow mechanics.

## Q2. Transfer restrictions (SafeTix, non-transferable tickets, artist-set limits) and feasibility for a third-party swap app

### Takeaway
Transfer on Ticketmaster, AXS and DICE is controlled by the ticketer and the event. Tickets can be mobile-only, have transfer delayed or disabled, or be resellable only through the official exchange. A third-party app therefore cannot guarantee delivery. It can only coordinate a transfer that the ticketer allows. New York is the main US jurisdiction that forces a transferable option.

### Cited Findings
- On TM FVE events, tickets are often mobile-only once the artist opts in. — [The Ticket Blog, Sept 2026](https://www.theticketblog.com/post/fan-to-fan-resale)
- On DICE, transfer to a friend is only possible "if that option is available for the show", which the organizer controls. — [DICE Help](https://dicefm.zendesk.com/hc/en-gb/articles/19958073128849-The-wait-list-explained)
- Ticketmaster changed transfer rules for Taylor Swift Eras Tour tickets in 2024 (Indianapolis/Toronto) amid reported ticket thefts, delaying transfers close to the show. — search-result titles from [CBC](https://www.cbc.ca/news/business/ticketmaster-taylor-swift-1.7346088) and [AOL/IndyStar](https://www.aol.com/ticketmasters-transfer-rule-know-taylor-155757323.html) (the page fetch was blocked, so the exact hour window was not verified)
- Ticketmaster has a "Ticket Transfer Troubleshooting" help page for cases where transfer is unavailable. — [TM Help](https://help.ticketmaster.com/hc/en-us/articles/37438737355537-Ticket-Transfer-Troubleshooting)
- **New York ACA §25.30:** operators may use paperless tickets only if consumers get "an option to purchase paperless tickets that the consumer can transfer at any price, and at any time, and without additional fees", or a form "transferrable independent of the operator". Prices must be the same regardless of transferability, and venues can't deny entry to a resold subscription or season ticket solely because it was resold. — [FindLaw ACA §25.30](https://codes.findlaw.com/ny/arts-and-cultural-affairs-law/aca-sect-25-30/)
- **Federal MAIN Event Ticketing Act (H.R. 2713):** as of 16 Sept 2026 it passed House Energy & Commerce 36-0 and would make circumvention of ticket issuers' "access controls" a BOTS Act violation. Resale advocates warn it would turn "private ticketing-platform rules" into federal law. — [TicketNews, Sept 2026](https://www.ticketnews.com/2026/09/main-event-ticketing-act-house-committee-bots-act/)

### Inferences
- **Technical feasibility:** SafeTix-style rotating barcodes mean a screenshot or PDF is worthless. The only valid hand-off is the ticketer's own "Transfer" (recipient email/phone) or the official exchange. Plus One should be a matching and escrow/commitment layer that tells the seller when to press "Transfer" in the TM/AXS/DICE app. It should confirm receipt with the buyer before releasing funds, and it should never ask for account credentials. Scraping or credential-sharing would risk ToS breaches and, if the MAIN Event Act passes, possible BOTS Act exposure.
- **Legal feasibility:** peer-to-peer transfer at face value is generally legal. Artist or ticketer terms, however, can disable transfer or restrict resale to official channels. In those cases the in-app answer should be to deep-link to the official Face Value Exchange or DICE wait list.
- "Friend transfer" (no money) is often allowed even when resale is not, but a payment moving through Plus One makes it a resale under most ToS.

### Gaps
- No quantitative source was found on the share of tours that restrict transfer or delay it (e.g., "72h before").
- The exact 2024 Eras transfer-delay window was not verified.
- The current status of NY §25.30 (it historically sunsets and is renewed; 2025-2026 bills S276 and S8221B exist) was not confirmed. — [NY Senate S276](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/bills/2025/S276)

## Q3. US legal landscape (federal, state) as of Sept 2026

### Takeaway
Federal action is about fee transparency and bots, not price caps. The FTC all-in pricing rule has been in force since 12 May 2025, the March 2025 executive order drives enforcement, the TICKET Act passed the House but not the Senate, and the MAIN Event Act is in committee stage. Price caps are happening at the state or district level: Maine (10% fee cap), Vermont (110% for small venues), DC (effective 2027). Massachusetts is pending, and many other cap bills failed.

### Cited Findings
- **FTC Rule on Unfair or Deceptive Fees:** covers live-event tickets and requires the total price, including mandatory fees, to be shown upfront. It took effect 12 May 2025. — [FTC press release, May 2025](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/05/ftc-rule-unfair-or-deceptive-fees-take-effect-may-12-2025); [FTC FAQ](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/rule-unfair-or-deceptive-fees-frequently-asked-questions). The first major enforcement was FTC v. StubHub on 9 Apr 2026. — [complaint](https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/StubHub-Complaint.pdf)
- **Executive Order "Combating Unfair Practices in the Live Entertainment Market" (31 Mar 2025):** directs the FTC to enforce the BOTS Act rigorously, with state AGs, and to consider price-transparency rulemaking. Treasury, the AG and the FTC owed a joint report and recommendations by 27 Sept 2025. It imposes no price caps. — [Wiley](https://www.wiley.law/alert-Executive-Order-on-Ticket-Resale-Market-Calls-for-Greater-FTC-Enforcement); [Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/full_text/xml/2025/04/03/2025-05906.xml)
- **TICKET Act (H.R.1402 / S.281, 119th Congress):** passed the House 409-15 in April 2025. As of January 2026 it was a "bipartisan Senate priority", and TicketNews (Aug 2026) does not report Senate passage. It covers all-in pricing, refunds, speculative listings and deceptive sites, not price caps. — [TicketNews, Aug 2026](https://www.ticketnews.com/2026/08/resale-industry-confronts-coordinated-statehouse-campaign-to-restrict-ticket-competition/); [Congress.gov S.281](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/senate-bill/281)
- **MAIN Event Ticketing Act:** the House E&C committee voted 36-0 on 16 Sept 2026. The Senate version, S.196, was reported by Senate Commerce in Sept 2025. — [TicketNews, Sept 2026](https://www.ticketnews.com/2026/09/main-event-ticketing-act-house-committee-bots-act/)
- **FTC enforcement against brokers and Live Nation:**
  - Key Investment Group was sued in Aug 2025 over 379,776 tickets; its motion to dismiss was denied in Apr 2026.
  - Elite Events settled in Jul 2026 for $300k paid, with a $10.7M penalty mostly suspended.
  - The FTC and 7 states are suing Live Nation/Ticketmaster over purchase-limit enforcement; that case is pending.
  - Source: [TicketNews, Sept 2026](https://www.ticketnews.com/2026/09/main-event-ticketing-act-house-committee-bots-act/)
- **Maine LD 913 (P.L. 2025, ch. 354):**
  - Caps resale marketplace charges at "10% of the total price of the original ticket, including taxes and fees".
  - Bans speculative tickets.
  - Mandates refunds for cancellations, counterfeits and non-delivery.
  - Individual (non-business) sellers are excluded from reseller rules.
  - Sources: [Maine AG consumer alert, 29 Jan 2026](https://www.maine.gov/ag/consumer-protection/consumer-issues-scam/consumer-alert-ticket-resales-thu-01292026-0751); signed June 2025 per [Pollstar](https://news.pollstar.com/2025/06/20/maine-governor-signs-ticketing-reform-with-10-resale-cap-spec-ban/)
  - The "10% resale cap" framing in press coverage vs. the AG's "marketplace fee cap" wording differs. Check the statute: [8 MRSA §1301](https://legislature.maine.gov/statutes/8/title8sec1301.html).
- **Vermont Act 109:** effective 1 Jul 2026. It caps resale at 110% of the original price for venues of 3,000 capacity or less and bans speculative listings. — [TicketNews, Aug 2026](https://www.ticketnews.com/2026/08/resale-industry-confronts-coordinated-statehouse-campaign-to-restrict-ticket-competition/)
- **DC RESALE Act:**
  - Final Council approval 16 Jul 2026, effective 1 Jan 2027.
  - Resale capped at 120% of face value including a maximum 10% fee; the mayor can adjust the cap to 0-20%.
  - Bans speculative and "surveillance" pricing and requires all-in pricing.
  - Resellers of 50+ tickets per year must register and post a $25k bond.
  - Covers live entertainment only, not sports.
  - Sources: [DC Council](https://dccouncil.gov/council-gives-final-approval-to-resale-act-regulating-ticket-sales-and-capping-ticket-resale-prices/). TicketNews describes it as a 10% cap plus a 10% fee (same arithmetic, different framing). — [TicketNews](https://www.ticketnews.com/2026/08/resale-industry-confronts-coordinated-statehouse-campaign-to-restrict-ticket-competition/)
- **Other states (TicketNews, Aug 2026):**
  - North Carolina (budget bill, effective 1 Oct 2026) requires resale marketplaces to link to the original issuer, all-in pricing, bot limits and a speculative-ticket ban.
  - Massachusetts' Senate version includes a 110% cap; it was in conference as of Aug 2026.
  - More than 100 ticket bills were introduced in 2026, and about 13 resale price-control proposals failed.
  - Source: [TicketNews](https://www.ticketnews.com/2026/08/resale-industry-confronts-coordinated-statehouse-campaign-to-restrict-ticket-competition/)
- California had two Ticketmaster-backed resale bills in 2026. — [CalMatters, Apr 2026](https://calmatters.org/digital-democracy/2026/04/ticketmaster-resale-market/) (outcome not verified)
- NIVA and artists released principles for revising New York ticketing laws in 2026. — [NIVA](https://www.nivassoc.org/statements/2026/artists-fans-indie-venues-and-leading-music-and-live-performance-organizations-release-principles-to-fix-new-york-ticketing-laws)

### Inferences
- A face-value-only swap app is structurally compliant with every cap law (Maine, Vermont, DC, and a possible Massachusetts cap). All-in fee display is mandatory under the FTC rule, so Plus One must show the total price, including its own fee, on the first screen.
- The regulatory trend (caps plus "link to original issuer") favors official or face-value exchanges. The MAIN Event Act, however, could make workarounds of ticketer controls federally risky.

### Gaps
- The outcome of the California 2026 bills was not verified.
- Canadian provincial law was not covered (Ontario's Ticket Sales Act and Quebec's resale rules were not verified in this research).
- Whether the joint EO report was published after Sept 2025, and what it contained, was not found.

## Q4. Lyte and other face-value startups: what happened, and lessons

### Takeaway
Lyte (founded 2014, about $53M raised) was an official, organizer-integrated fan-to-fan exchange. It drifted into primary ticketing and into quietly reselling VIP tickets for promoters at a markup, bought the troubled Festicket, and went dark in mid-Sept 2024 owing promoters money. Its failure shows the dependence on integration, the cost of acquiring customers, and how weak face-value margins are.

### Cited Findings
- Founded in 2014 by Ant Taylor, Lyte raised about $53M over four rounds; investors included Chamath Palihapitiya (Social Capital) and Joseph Edelman. The site went dark in mid-Sept 2024, although it was still announcing clients on 9 Sept. — [Billboard, Sept 2024](https://www.billboard.com/pro/ticketing-company-lyte-shuts-down/); [Digital Music News, 17 Sept 2024](https://www.digitalmusicnews.com/2024/09/17/after-53-million-in-funding-is-lyte-shutting-down/)
- Lyte evolved to help promoters "scalp their high-end tickets and VIP festival tickets" while splitting profits with organizers. Partners included Pitchfork, Newport Folk, Lost Lands and Baja Beach Fest. — [Billboard](https://www.billboard.com/pro/ticketing-company-lyte-shuts-down/)
- Promoters are owed "hundreds of thousands of dollars"; one attorney reported more than $1M in cumulative claims. CEO Taylor resigned and creditors sought a buyer. — [Billboard](https://www.billboard.com/pro/ticketing-company-lyte-shuts-down/); [DMN, 23 Sept 2024](https://www.digitalmusicnews.com/2024/09/23/lyte-meltdown-update/)
- Two festivals sued over about $300K in losses. — [TicketNews, Sept 2024](https://www.ticketnews.com/2024/09/two-festivals-sue-ticketing-site-lyte-after-abrupt-shutdown-300k-losses/). Lyte was the official exchange for Bourbon & Beyond. — [WHAS11](https://www.whas11.com/article/entertainment/events/lyte-tickets-website-goes-dark-what-to-do-if-you-have-bourbon-and-beyond-tickets/417-77578c30-acfe-4276-b57e-c6c689fe3846)
- Hypebot's analysis of why Lyte failed:
  - rising customer-acquisition costs;
  - organizers restricting inventory through technology;
  - scrutiny of fees;
  - the Festicket acquisition (a financially troubled UK firm with weaker technology);
  - entering primary ticketing turned partners into competitors.
  - Source: [Hypebot, Oct 2024](https://www.hypebot.com/hypebot/2024/10/the-lyte-bankruptcy-and-what-it-reveals-about-the-ticketing-industry.html)
- Twickets announced a US launch in 2017 and still lists relatively few US events in 2026. — [TicketNews 2017](https://www.ticketnews.com/2017/06/twickets-launching-united-states/); [twickets.live/en/us](https://www.twickets.live/en/us)

### Inferences
- **Lessons for Plus One:**
  1. **Liquidity and cold start.** Face-value supply appears only when a fan can't go, so match rates per show are low. The buddy-finding feature could supply the engagement that pure resale lacks.
  2. **Integration dependence.** Official exchanges get the inventory, but ticketers can switch partners off or build their own (TM FVE).
  3. **Thin margins.** Take rates capped near 10% make paid acquisition uneconomic. Grow through artist and fan communities (CashorTrade's jam-band model) rather than ads.
  4. **Trust.** Hold funds in escrow and never co-mingle them; Lyte's collapse stranded promoter money.
  5. **Mission drift.** Monetizing markups destroys the face-value brand.

### Gaps
- Lyte's formal bankruptcy filing details and date were not verified.
- No sourced details were found on other US face-value startups (e.g., funding for Tixel's US expansion, or CashorTrade's scale).

## Q5. Share of tickets resold, markups, and how often fans sell at face value

### Takeaway
Hard, current, sourced figures were largely not obtained in this research round. The available signals: StubHub's total price can run 25% above the advertised price because of fees alone (FTC), organizers such as Oasis cancel resale tickets to fight scalping, and Billboard reports that Eras Tour fans themselves were the biggest resellers.

### Cited Findings
- FTC example: the StubHub total was "25% higher than the price initially advertised" (fees only, not markup). — [FTC complaint, Apr 2026](https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/StubHub-Complaint.pdf)
- Oasis planned to cancel 50,000 resale tickets as part of a scalping crackdown (UK/Ireland 2025 dates; this comes from the headline only). — [Music Business Worldwide](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/oasis-to-cancel-50000-resale-tickets-amid-tour-scalping-crackdown/)
- Billboard's headline says "Taylor Swift's Eras Tour Fans Were Its Biggest Ticket Scalper"; the body was paywalled and not verified. — [Billboard](https://www.billboard.com/pro/taylor-swift-eras-tour-fans-biggest-ticket-scalper/)
- Eras resale prices "soar" on the secondary market (Dec 2022). — [Axios](https://www.axios.com/2022/12/02/taylor-swift-resale-tickets-eras-tour) (the specific figures were not retrieved)

### Inferences
- Fans who resell tend to capture the markup when they can. Face-value selling happens mainly where it is forced (FVE opt-in, DICE, caps) or where community norms enforce it (CashorTrade).

### Gaps
- None of the following were found: NITO or VPA data on the share of tickets resold, average markups for top shows, Oasis North America 2025 resale prices, or the percentage of fan sellers who choose face value.
- StubHub S-1/10-Q and Vivid Seats 10-K figures (GMS, take rate) were not retrieved.
- A follow-up should pull these filings from SEC EDGAR.
