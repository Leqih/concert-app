# North American Live-Music Fan Behavior: Solo, Groups, Spare Tickets, Price Sensitivity (quantitative, with segment splits)

Scope note: US/Canada unless labeled. Fieldwork current to Sept 2026 as found. Every figure below was read from the fetched page named, except where marked "[derived]" or "[unverified]". Items the brief already had (Pollstar, Bandsintown 2022, viagogo UK, Ticketmaster festival solo, SeatGeek FL, Deloitte 2024, CouponCabin, Gallup 20%) are not repeated.

## 1. Attendance scale and trend (Live Nation, 2019–2025) plus generational attendance/spend

### Takeaway
Live Nation fan counts rose from about 98M (2019, derived) to 121M (2022), 151M (2024) and 159M (2025), a chartable series. Ticketmaster fee-bearing tickets went from 280M (2022) to 346M (2025). Gen Z and millennials attend most often and spend most.

### Cited Findings
- **2022:** 121M fans at 43,600 events in 45 countries, stated as +24% vs 2019. Fee-bearing tickets were 280M (+28% vs 2019) and total tickets processed about 551M. Ancillary spend per fan was up at least 20% vs 2019 across venue types, and US entry prices were held under $35. — [Live Nation FY2022 results](https://newsroom.livenation.com/news/live-nation-entertainment-reports-fourth-quarter-full-year-2022-results/)
- **2019 [derived]:** 121M ÷ 1.24 ≈ 98M fans, and 280M ÷ 1.28 ≈ 219M fee-bearing tickets. Both are computed from the percentage changes in the FY2022 release.
- **2024:** 151M fans. — [Live Nation FY2024 exhibit 99.1 (SEC)](https://investors.livenationentertainment.com/sec-filings/all-sec-filings/content/0001335258-25-000027/lyv-2024q4xex991er.htm); [MBW](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/live-nation-saw-151m-fans-attend-its-events-in-2024-as-revenue-rose-3-to-23bn/)
- **2023 [unverified]:** 145M fans. This comes from memory and a search snippet; I did not fetch the page. Verify at the [FY2023 release](https://news.livenationentertainment.com/news/live-nation-entertainment-reports-full-year-and-fourth-quarter-2023-results).
- **2025:** 159M fans (+5% YoY) at about 55,000 shows. International fans outnumbered US fans for the first time, and North American stadium fans more than doubled. Venue Nation hosted 65M fans (+8%), with more than 70M guided for 2026. Ticketmaster fee-bearing tickets were 346M (+2%; concerts +4%). Event-related deferred revenue was $4B (+21%). More than 80% of large-venue 2026 shows were booked, pacing up double digits in North America. — [Live Nation FY2025 results (Feb 2026)](https://news.livenationentertainment.com/news/live-nation-entertainment-full-year-and-fourth-quarter-2025-results)
- **Fans per show [derived]:** 2022: 121M/43,600 ≈ 2,775 fans per event. 2025: 159M/55,000 ≈ 2,890 fans per event. These are rough ratios because the event definitions may differ.
- **2025 H1 pace:** Live Nation sold more than 130M tickets in the seven months through July 2025 (+6% YoY). — [eMarketer citing Live Nation Q2 2025](https://www.emarketer.com/content/2025-will-record-year-concertgoing)
- **Concerts per year by segment (LendingTree 2022):** concertgoers averaged 4 shows, up from 3 in 2021. Gen Z averaged 4. By gender, men averaged 3 and women 1 (medians/averages as the page reports them; the gap looks large, so treat with caution). Sample: n=2,072 US consumers, May 6–10, 2022. — [LendingTree](https://www.lendingtree.com/credit-cards/study/concert-plans-budgets/)
- **Share spending $500+ per season (LendingTree 2022):** 32% of concertgoers, versus 22% pre-pandemic; 41% of Gen Z. Willingness to pay for a favorite artist was $328 overall and $398 for Gen Z. — [LendingTree 2022](https://www.lendingtree.com/credit-cards/study/concert-plans-budgets/)
- **LendingTree 2025:** 51% plan to attend a concert or festival in summer/fall 2025. Estimated annual spend averaged $992 per person. The most spent on a single ticket averaged $237, and willingness to pay for a favorite artist averaged $287. Sample: n=2,050 US adults 18–79, June 12–14, 2025 (QuestionPro, nonprobability sample with quotas). Generations defined as Gen Z 18–28, millennial 29–44, Gen X 45–60, boomer 61–79. — [LendingTree 2025 Concert Spending Report](https://www.lendingtree.com/credit-cards/study/concert-spending-report/)
- **Plan-to-attend series (LendingTree):** 26% planned summer concerts in 2022 (plus 12% in fall). 51% planned summer/fall in 2025. Question wording differs between years, so this is not a clean series.
- **Credit Karma (n=1,006 US adults, Sept 1–4, 2023, Qualtrics), share whose monthly concert spend rose since pre-pandemic:** Gen Z 42%, millennials 34%, Gen X 19%, boomers+ 11%. 90% of Gen Z spend on entertainment monthly, and 19% spend more than $300 per month. — [Credit Karma](https://creditkarma.com/about/commentary/gen-z-opens-their-wallets-as-the-cost-of-entertainment-rises)
- **Travel (Bank of America 2025 Summer Travel & Entertainment survey, via eMarketer):** 50% of respondents traveled out of state or abroad for a concert or event in the past two years. The figure was 70% for millennials and about two-thirds for Gen Z. — [eMarketer](https://www.emarketer.com/content/2025-will-record-year-concertgoing)
- **CivicScience 2025:** Gen Z and millennials are the most likely concertgoers, and Gen Z is the most likely to attend 3+ concerts. The share planning to cut concert spending fell 6 points vs 2024. No sample size is given. — [CivicScience](https://civicscience.com/summer-concerts-trends-in-2025-gen-z-and-buy-now-pay-later-take-center-stage/)

### Inferences
- Attendance keeps growing, and fans per event are roughly flat at about 2.8–2.9K. The industry is growing through more fans and more shows, not through bigger crowds per show.
- Travel for shows (50–70% among young adults) means more fans end up in a city where their own friends aren't. That supports the "crew for this show" need.

### Gaps
- I did not verify the 2023 Live Nation fan count on the primary page. The 2019 figure is derived, not sourced.
- I found no fetched primary source for the "share of Gen Z who attended live music in the last 12 months." Luminate year-end/midyear reports were not retrieved.

## 2. Solo attendance and single-ticket purchasing

### Takeaway
I found no new US survey with gender or age splits on solo concert attendance. Public single-ticket share data beyond the SeatGeek Florida numbers the brief already has was not available from Ticketmaster, StubHub, Vivid Seats, Eventbrite or DICE in what I could retrieve.

### Cited Findings
- **Eventbrite Social Study 2026:** n=4,051 aged 18–35 (US 2,028; UK 2,023), fieldwork July 11–16, 2025. 79% plan to attend more events in 2026. 79% value spontaneity over a perfect plan, yet 51% still need detailed logistics. 58% prefer events where socializing isn't the main focus. 89% want events that connect them to their community. 69% rely on personal networks or word of mouth for discovery. 55% say affordability is very important. Nearly 25% of 18–29-year-olds report feeling lonely. Results are combined US+UK, and the release gives no solo-attendance or gender split. — [Eventbrite press release, Jan 14 2026](https://www.eventbrite.com/blog/press/newsroom/eventbrites-inaugural-social-study-report-reveals-the-reset-to-real/)

### Inferences
- "Socializing isn't the main focus" (58%) together with "connect to community" (89%) suggests young adults want company framed around the show rather than explicit "meet people" events. That supports buddy-matching anchored on a specific show rather than a social-meetup framing.

### Gaps
- No US solo-attendance survey with gender/age splits was found. The Ticketmaster, StubHub, Vivid Seats and DICE single-ticket shares are not in any public release I found; searches returned only guides and blogs. The Vivid Seats 10-K and StubHub S-1 were not fetched and may hold orders and tickets-sold figures that would give an average tickets per order.
- No Google Trends "concert alone" series was retrieved. Pulling one directly would be possible and would give a time series.

## 3. Group coordination, spare tickets and resale

### Takeaway
The data here is thin. The best numbers are on secondary-market use and scams, not on plans falling through or unused tickets.

### Cited Findings
- **LendingTree 2025 (n=2,050, June 2025):** 30% bought tickets through resellers. 25% experienced ticket scams or counterfeits, 14% of them more than once. 27% signed up for a credit card specifically for presale access. — [LendingTree 2025](https://www.lendingtree.com/credit-cards/study/concert-spending-report/)
- **LendingTree 2022:** 20% would buy early-bird festival tickets without knowing the lineup, to get lower prices. — [CNBC on LendingTree 2022](https://www.cnbc.com/2022/07/07/26-percent-of-concertgoers-plan-to-take-on-debt-to-attend-shows-this-summer.html)

### Inferences
- A 25% scam-exposure rate is a strong argument for verified, face-value fan-to-fan transfers, which is Plus One's swap feature.

### Gaps
- I found no survey measuring how often group plans fall through, how many fans ended up with an unused or spare ticket, or resale price versus face value. Average tickets per order was also not found in any public source.

## 4. Loneliness and friendship by age and gender (US)

### Takeaway
Loneliness is concentrated in the young. In Cigna's 2024 data it runs from 67% (Gen Z) down to 44% (boomers). Gallup finds young US men (15–34) are the loneliest group at 25% daily, well above the OECD median. Pew finds under-50s have fewer close friends and are more often lonely.

### Cited Findings
- **Cigna (Vitality Index, n>5,000 US adults 18+, May 29–June 14, 2024; report published 2025), share lonely:** Gen Z 67%, millennials 65%, Gen X 60%, boomers 44%. The report gives no gender split and no trend. — [Cigna Group 2025 Loneliness in America report (PDF)](https://filecache.mediaroom.com/mr5mr_thecignagroup/183661/2025-loneliness-in-america-report-the-cigna-group.pdf)
- **Gallup (about 1,000 US adults per year, phone; 2024 fieldwork June 28–Aug 1; data pooled 2023–2024), daily loneliness:**

  | Age | Men | Women |
  |---|---|---|
  | 15–34 | 25% | 18% |
  | 35–54 | 15% | 20% |
  | 55+ | 16% | 17% |

  The national average is 18%. For young men, the US (25%) compares with an OECD median of 15%, Canada 22%, France 24% and Türkiye 30%. — [Gallup, May 2025](https://news.gallup.com/poll/690788/younger-men-among-loneliest-west.aspx)
- **Pew (n=5,057, July 17–23, 2023):** 8% of Americans have no close friends, 53% have 1–4, and 38% have 5+. The share with 5+ close friends by age is under 30: 32%, 30–49: 34%, 50–64: 40%, 65+: 49%. By gender, men are at 40% with 5+ and 50% with 1–4; women are at 36% with 5+ and 55% with 1–4. — [Pew, Oct 2023](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2023/10/12/what-does-friendship-look-like-in-america/)
- **Pew (n=6,204, Sept 3–15, 2024):** 16% feel lonely or isolated all or most of the time, with roughly equal shares of men and women. 22% of under-50s often feel lonely vs 9% of those 50+. — [Pew, Jan 2025](https://www.pewresearch.org/social-trends/2025/01/16/men-women-and-social-connections/)

### Inferences
- Plus One's core demographic (18–34) has both the highest attendance and the highest loneliness and smallest friend circles. Young men are a notable underserved segment on the loneliness side, while women dominate the safety concern (section 5).

### Gaps
- There is no Cigna time series (2018/2021/2024) in the 2025 report. An APA poll was not retrieved.

## 5. Women's safety at live music (US)

### Takeaway
The first peer-reviewed US prevalence study found that 82% of women (vs 39% of men) had experienced sexual harassment or assault at live music events. 88% of incidents went unreported.

### Cited Findings
- **Injury Prevention (BMJ), published Sept 16, 2025, DOI 10.1136/ip-2025-045809:** based on the GrooveSafe Fan Experience Survey 2024. n=1,091 US adults who attended at least one gig in the past year; 51% women, 66% aged 30–49.
  - 61% overall experienced inappropriate sexual behavior: women 82%, men 39%. 20% of women said it happened often.
  - Women reported more incidents in large arenas.
  - More women experienced harassment when attending **with friends or partners**. (This is notable: going with others is not necessarily protective.)
  - 88% did not report the incident to staff. The most common reason was "nothing would be done" (25%).

  — [BMJ Group press release](https://bmjgroup.com/6-in-10-us-music-fans-say-they-have-been-sexually-harassed-assaulted-at-a-live-gig-survey-suggests/); [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40983536/)
- **Canada:** a CBC-reported survey says more than half of women were harassed at outdoor festivals. It is older, and I did not fetch the details. — [CBC](https://amp.cbc.ca/lite/story/1.4144450)

### Inferences
- Safety features such as verified profiles, women-only crew options and meeting before the venue are table stakes for buddy matching aimed at women. The "with friends" finding means safety messaging should not assume that company equals safety.

### Gaps
- I found no US survey on the share of women who avoid going to concerts alone. The viagogo UK data the brief already has remains the closest.

## 6. Price sensitivity and debt

### Takeaway
Across LendingTree (2022, 2025), Credit Karma (2023), Cash App and CivicScience, a third or more of Gen Z and millennials use debt or BNPL for concerts. More than half of Americans have skipped favorite artists because of price.

### Cited Findings
- **LendingTree 2025 (n=2,050, June 12–14, 2025):**
  - 53% have skipped a favorite artist because of high prices.
  - 57% are reconsidering attendance because of the economy.
  - 23% of Americans have used BNPL for concert expenses: Gen Z 37%, millennials 35%, Gen X 19%, boomers 3%, parents of young children 44%.
  - 31% of concertgoers expect to incur debt, plus 8% who say maybe.

  — [LendingTree](https://www.lendingtree.com/credit-cards/study/concert-spending-report/)
- **LendingTree 2022 (n=2,072, May 6–10, 2022):** 26% of concertgoers planned to take on debt, vs 12% "historically": Gen Z 41%, millennials 31%. 25% of Gen Z and 27% of millennials say the debt is "definitely worth it." — [LendingTree 2022](https://www.lendingtree.com/credit-cards/study/concert-plans-budgets/). **Conflict:** [CNBC's writeup](https://www.cnbc.com/2022/07/07/26-percent-of-concertgoers-plan-to-take-on-debt-to-attend-shows-this-summer.html) gives "Gen Z 25% / millennials 27% willing to go into debt." That appears to confuse the "definitely worth it" figures with the plan-debt figures, so prefer the primary page.
- **Debt series (LendingTree, share of concertgoers expecting debt):** 12% historical baseline, 26% in 2022, 31% (+8% maybe) in 2025. Wording differs between years.
- **Credit Karma (n=1,006, Sept 1–4, 2023), willing to take on debt for entertainment:** Gen Z 35%, millennials 33%, Gen X 16%, boomers+ 6%. Among Gen Z, the sacrifices made for live-event tickets were: cut dining out 35%, borrowed from savings 30%, took on credit card debt 21%, sacrificed food or necessities 19%. A third of Gen Z feel social-media pressure to spend money they don't have on live events. — [Credit Karma](https://creditkarma.com/about/commentary/gen-z-opens-their-wallets-as-the-cost-of-entertainment-rises)
- **Cash App survey (via eMarketer, 2025):** 31% of Gen Z used BNPL for concert tickets, and more than 50% used it for related costs such as lodging, transport and outfits. The sample size was not given in the secondary source. — [eMarketer](https://www.emarketer.com/content/2025-will-record-year-concertgoing)
- **CivicScience 2025:** 12% of ticket buyers are "very likely" and 24% "somewhat likely" to use BNPL. No sample size is given. — [CivicScience](https://civicscience.com/summer-concerts-trends-in-2025-gen-z-and-buy-now-pay-later-take-center-stage/)
- **Coachella (Billboard via eMarketer):** about 60% of GA buyers used the payment plan, with the lowest upfront payment at $49.99. — [eMarketer](https://www.emarketer.com/content/2025-will-record-year-concertgoing)
- **An unattributed "1,000 Gen Z" survey:** 86% overspent on live events, with FOMO the number one reason. The publisher is not named, so it should not be used without tracing it. — [Morning Brew, Mar 2025](https://www.morningbrew.com/stories/2025/03/05/the-newest-gen-z-plight-concert-tickets)

### Inferences
- A generational gradient appears across three independent sources (LendingTree 2025, Credit Karma 2023, LendingTree 2022): Gen Z ≈ millennials > Gen X ≫ boomers. It can be charted as a grouped bar.
- High skip rates (53%) alongside heavy BNPL use suggest demand is limited by price, not interest. A face-value swap directly addresses that for the spare-ticket supply.

### Gaps
- I found no survey on "would attend more at face value." The Bankrate concert-specific survey was not found; Bankrate's pages found were general discretionary-spending surveys. The Billboard roundup of spending studies was paywalled (HTTP 402). The Cash App primary release was not fetched, so its sample and date are unknown.
